Who We Are

Introduction

MakerGhat, a Section 8 non-profit organisation, champions grassroots innovation and entrepreneurship throughout India, collaborating with prestigious institutions like Stanford University, Georgia Tech, and IIT-Bombay. We aim to provide young individuals with a sense of agency through hands-on making and tinkering.

School Partnerships

By deploying specialised curriculums, teacher training, and evaluation frameworks focused on practical skill development, we aim to foster a culture of experiential learning. Our initiatives are particularly tailored for youth in poverty-stricken communities, who have generationally felt disempowered. Through tangible hands-on learning experiences, we strive to instill a belief in these students that they can enact meaningful change with self-developed solutions.

The program exposes students to a variety of materials and processes, emphasising activity-based learning that is self-directed and promotes teamwork. This hands-on approach not only sparks curiosity and interest but also enhances critical thinking, problem-solving, self-efficacy, and communication and collaboration—core components of employability skills. By encouraging students to question, plan, iterate, and reflect, MakerGhat aims to nurture innovative thinking and equip them with the tools to tackle real-world problems creatively.

The Challenge

The Problem & The Moment

Robotics contests such as the World Robot Olympiad, FIRST LEGO League, and FIRST Global Challenge decide who gets fast‑tracked into top universities and R&D labs. India, despite its massive talent pool, sends only a handful of teams.

"In 2024, India finished fourth at FIRST Global—encouraging, but still shy of the podium."

If Maharashtra’s strong legacy in education and innovation has shaped national progress, robotics can put the state on the global map—we simply need a stronger, highly-supported funnel.

Our Solution

Our Answer

Project SHAKTI will identify fifty high‑potential students (twenty‑five two‑person teams) each year, give them 18 months of deep coaching, and back them all the way to national and global finals. MakerGhat’s 25,000‑school network supplies the talent; a new fellowship structure supplies the lift.

📍

One Physical Hub

Workshops and bootcamps will run at IIT Bombay, Mumbai—a long‑time MakerGhat partner offering labs, dorms, and faculty support. (MUSO Mumbai remains available for future cohorts.)

🌐

Hybrid Delivery

Weekly skill “sprints” happen seamlessly online, while district scrimmages and state qualifiers rotate through dynamic regional meet‑ups.

⚡

Guaranteed Women’s Track

A minimum of half the seats—and at least three of ten global‑finalist slots—are reserved for girls. Separate mentoring circles address retention and confidence gaps.
